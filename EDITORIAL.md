# Editorial compass

## The promise

Give readers the vocabulary to make intentional creative decisions and direct AI with the precision of an artist working a material.

## The voice

Curious, concrete, playful, and intelligent. Assume a new reader can handle serious ideas. Begin with a scene or a puzzle. Explain the vocabulary just when it becomes useful. Let a white T-shirt do surprisingly interesting work.

## A chapter's possible rhythm

A small scene → a useful concept → the same shirt viewed differently → a precise prompt → a before/after comparison → a question worth carrying away.

This is a writing aid, not a mandatory template for every chapter.

## The framework

- Persuasion: what response are we inviting?
- Archetype: what meaning or identity are we expressing?
- Visual language: how should that meaning look and feel?
- Sculpting with AI: what changes, what stays, and how will we judge the result?

## Evidence

Use original sources where available. Distinguish an author's account of a theory from independent evidence supporting it. Distinguish a historical artifact from a curator's interpretation of it. Place citations next to factual claims and list research starting points on the source shelf.

Hypothetical brands and prompts should be explicitly invented. A change in presentation never establishes a new product fact.

## Scope for now

A book skeleton and working reading site. Seven chapter outlines establish a path from meaning to implementation. The existing book is background; expand and improve one chapter at a time with the author. Keep schedules, grading, and classroom mechanics out of the book.

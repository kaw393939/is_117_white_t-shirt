# The Plain White T-Shirt: editorial prospectus

Status: first-layer structural draft, prepared 2026-09-23. This is a production blueprint, not a finished manuscript or a verified research review. Existing published chapter paths remain unchanged until the migration issue is reviewed.

## Promise and audience

Start with a white T-shirt. Learn to see more, ask better questions, direct AI, and turn deliberate choices into a coherent shopping experience. The intended reader is a beginning web professional or creative practitioner; programming expertise is not assumed. The final outcome is a complete HTML/CSS ecommerce prototype with a brand rationale, customer journey, discovery plan, and test evidence.

## Authorial thesis

Faster production makes imagination, articulate questions, cultural literacy, technical understanding, and critical judgment increasingly consequential. This is the book’s argument to explore, not a claim that implementation skills are obsolete. Natural language can direct work, but explanations must survive comparison and observation. Every consequential choice should be explainable, open to challenge, and revisable. Intuition and experimentation may precede an explanation.

## Shared constraint and continuity

The garment is white. A logo, image, wordmark or identifier may appear on the front, back, sleeve, hem or another justified location. Scale, typography, placement and production method are creative choices. Distinguish changing presentation around an identical product from changing the garment itself. Keep a fixed fictional product record for controlled comparisons; record intentional departures.

Retain Standard Issue, Drift & Thread and Blank Canvas Co. as the working brand trio. Existing references to Blank Riot require an explicit continuity decision before revision. Build one complete reference shop first; use the other brands for contrasting examples rather than maintaining three independent full stores.

## Narrative method

A human problem or artifact opens a question. Vocabulary makes it discussable. Alternatives expose choices. A decision is explained, challenged, tested and revised. Historical and personal stories recur when they deepen that question. Personal recollections must come from the author; do not fabricate interviews or anecdotes. Real brand cases distinguish company claims, an author’s interpretation and independently supported facts, with period context.

Experts supply sourced questions, not simulated endorsements. The Hero and the Outlaw, Starbucks, Helvetica, and Where Wizards Stay Up Late are research and discussion candidates, not a verified source dossier. Wikipedia can orient readers; claim-level evidence comes from appropriate original work and authoritative records. Museum interpretation and primary artifacts are labeled separately. Videos are optional; essential explanations remain in the text.

## Working size and boundaries

Six parts and 24 short chapters, with an initial editorial target of 45,000–55,000 words before appendices. Treat the target as adjustable after a finished sample. Keep current platform procedures in dated companion material. No classroom administration, grading, live payments, or comprehensive computing survey. Every historical detour must return to a decision about the shirt, the shop, or its business.

## Opening and closing

Opening: put a white shirt on a white surface; compare a tiny identifier with a larger mark and glimpse the finished shop. Closing: return to that surface and ask what the reader can now notice, explain, test, and transfer to another project.

## Chapter briefs

Each brief is a first-layer content contract. Historical assertions, named cases, quotations and tool instructions require verification in the research pass before expansion. Chapter IDs below are planning IDs, not new published URLs.

### Part 1: Learn to see and ask

#### 01. Every mark is a choice

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A tiny hem identifier and a chest-sized graphic on the same white garment.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Explain a choice through intention, context, consequence, and evidence; allow intuition before explanation.
- **Vocabulary:** constraint, placement, scale, negative space, rationale.
- **Planned demonstration:** A labeled shirt-placement comparison.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 1; source historical influences only when used. Distinguish a fixed-product presentation experiment from altering the product.
- **Boundary:** No universal prescription for what a rebellious brand must look like.

#### 02. What becomes valuable when making gets faster?

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** An impressive generated design whose director cannot explain its choices.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Distinguish output fluency from understanding; identify what can be delegated and what requires investigation.
- **Vocabulary:** delegation, iteration, judgment, verification.
- **Planned demonstration:** Two revision paths: unexamined generation and informed comparison.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Author perspective on education; research any labor-history claims before using the horse/car analogy as evidence.
- **Boundary:** Do not predict job displacement or declare implementation knowledge obsolete.

#### 03. Borrow an expert’s questions

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Two reviewers disagree about the same checkout.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Translate a sourced principle into an explicit question; compare usability, maintainability, correctness, and expression.
- **Vocabulary:** primary source, interpretation, authority, tradeoff.
- **Planned demonstration:** An expert-lens card with a documented idea and a separately labeled application.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Research Krug, Knuth, Robert C. Martin and other relevant practitioners through their own work; opinions are not universal rules.
- **Boundary:** Do not invent quotations or simulate an expert endorsement.

#### 04. Learn the material: working with LLMs

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A model confidently adds an unsupported performance claim to shirt copy.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Supply context, examples and constraints; recognize uncertainty, instruction/data boundaries, tool use, and verification limits.
- **Vocabulary:** model, token, context, instruction, tool, hallucination, prompt injection.
- **Planned demonstration:** An annotated brief and a failure/revision pair.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Coordinate existing #72; current official technical documentation and original research where appropriate; distinguish provider-specific behavior.
- **Boundary:** No exhaustive API tutorial or promise that prompting guarantees truth.

### Part 2: Give the shirt meaning

#### 05. The client asked for a website

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A fictional shop owner asks for a redesign before explaining the business problem.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Discover business needs, audit an existing presence, define outcomes and scope, and separate influence from guarantees.
- **Vocabulary:** discovery, brief, scope, stakeholder, baseline.
- **Planned demonstration:** A fictional client brief and current-presence map.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Label the owner and observations invented; source professional guidance where claims require it.
- **Boundary:** Do not guarantee customers or assume redesign is always the answer.

#### 06. Who is this for?

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Two people like the same graphic but face different purchasing constraints.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Build a provisional persona around goals and circumstances; distinguish research from assumption and personas from archetypes.
- **Vocabulary:** persona, interview, assumption, need, segment.
- **Planned demonstration:** A persona with an evidence/unknowns column.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Official or original UX research; no fabricated interviews or demographic stereotypes.
- **Boundary:** No personality diagnosis or invented customer testimony.

#### 07. Why we say yes—and when we should say no

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A useful fit explanation beside a resetting scarcity timer.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Analyze persuasion and framing, identify claim obligations, and preserve freedom to decline.
- **Vocabulary:** framing, reciprocity, social proof, scarcity, authority, unity.
- **Planned demonstration:** A messaging comparison with ethical consequences.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 2; #37 research, #61 figure; primary studies distinguished from Cialdini’s summary.
- **Boundary:** No universal strongest-principle ranking or fictional sales statistics presented as evidence.

#### 08. Who do you get to be?

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Three brands offer different identities through one white shirt.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Connect positioning, story and voice while keeping archetypes flexible and culturally situated.
- **Vocabulary:** positioning, archetype, voice, identity, cultural context.
- **Planned demonstration:** A story/accent/failure-mode card.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 3; #38 and #62; research The Hero and the Outlaw and Starbucks cases with publication-period context.
- **Boundary:** Do not turn a book’s brand interpretation into a timeless fact.

### Part 3: Make meaning visible

#### 09. Letters have voices

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Identical shirt copy set in contrasting typographic treatments.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Explain type, hierarchy, spacing, measure and readability; connect typography to context without rigid personality labels.
- **Vocabulary:** typeface, font, weight, leading, tracking, kerning, measure.
- **Planned demonstration:** Controlled typography comparisons with unchanged wording.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Helvetica viewing guide and verified artifact records; film statements are attributed viewpoints.
- **Boundary:** Do not rely on film access to teach the essential concept.

#### 10. Color does a job

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A secondary action attracts more attention than the purchase decision.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Assign color roles, compare value and saturation, check contrast, and question universal color symbolism.
- **Vocabulary:** hue, value, saturation, contrast, palette, gamut.
- **Planned demonstration:** Palette role comparison and accessible text examples.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Official accessibility guidance; source any cultural or perceptual claims; acknowledge screen/print differences.
- **Boundary:** No unsourced color-psychology rules or color-only instructions.

#### 11. Arrange the surface

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** The same content feels confusing, calm, or energetic after rearrangement.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Use alignment, proximity, whitespace and grids; compare proportional systems against actual content.
- **Vocabulary:** grid, column, gutter, alignment, proximity, proportion, golden ratio.
- **Planned demonstration:** Equal divisions, thirds and golden-ratio layouts, plus one purposeful grid break.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Document historical examples through collection records and practitioner work.
- **Boundary:** Do not claim a universal beauty law or invent golden-ratio overlays on historical objects.

#### 12. Pictures, marks, and visual arguments

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A sleeve mark and a cropped chest graphic express the same intention differently.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Choose imagery, crop, scale, placement and production method; document what changes in the garment.
- **Vocabulary:** wordmark, symbol, crop, texture, embroidery, print, identifier.
- **Planned demonstration:** Annotated garment front/back/sleeve/hem treatments.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing dossiers; image rights and provenance; technical production claims need actual sources.
- **Boundary:** Keep white fabric as the constraint; avoid implying decoration has no manufacturing consequences.

#### 13. Design has a history—and arguments

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Two historical objects complicate the labels applied to them.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Read artifact, maker, institution and audience together; borrow a technique deliberately and explain limits.
- **Vocabulary:** modernism, postmodernism, quotation, reduction, hierarchy.
- **Planned demonstration:** Historical artifact paired with an original shirt reconstruction.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 4; #39 and #63; distinguish museum interpretation from historical documents.
- **Boundary:** No simple orderly-modernist versus chaotic-postmodern binary.

### Part 4: Turn intention into an experience

#### 14. Sculpting with AI

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A promising first draft is damaged by an indiscriminate revision.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Generate alternatives, identify what works, preserve constraints, change deliberately, and retain versions.
- **Vocabulary:** iteration, invariant, comparison, revision, acceptance criteria.
- **Planned demonstration:** A prompt/output/decision sequence including a rejected alternative.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 5; #64; label invented outputs or preserve actual provenance when generated.
- **Boundary:** No staged success portrayed as a measured experiment.

#### 15. A brand someone else can build

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A collaborator interprets an ambiguous brand guide.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Make strategy executable through voice rules, tokens, components, examples and exceptions.
- **Vocabulary:** token, component, style guide, handoff, exception.
- **Planned demonstration:** Guide-to-component-to-page handoff.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 6, style guide and dossiers; #65; official design-system documentation.
- **Boundary:** Do not equate consistency with making every page identical.

#### 16. Follow the customer

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A shopper returns a shirt after a seemingly successful conversion.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Map pre-purchase, purchase and post-purchase needs; compare journey and funnel without reducing people to metrics.
- **Vocabulary:** touchpoint, journey, funnel, friction, retention.
- **Planned demonstration:** Journey and funnel aligned around the same fictional scenario.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Persona from Chapter 6; distinguish hypothesized behavior from observed research.
- **Boundary:** No invented funnel conversion rates represented as analytics.

#### 17. Make the next step understandable

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A shopper cannot find sizing or determine the complete price.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Design navigation, forms and feedback; observe usability early; account for keyboard and assistive-technology use.
- **Vocabulary:** information architecture, navigation, affordance, feedback, label, usability.
- **Planned demonstration:** Sitemap, wireframe and observed revision.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Krug’s own materials and W3C/WAI; actual observations or explicitly scripted demonstration.
- **Boundary:** Do not equate attractive visuals with usability or accessibility.

### Part 5: Understand and build the medium

#### 18. From signals to networks

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** The shirt photograph must travel between machines.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Explain information, circuit and packet switching, shared networks and protocol responsibilities at a useful introductory level.
- **Vocabulary:** bit, signal, bandwidth, circuit, packet, protocol.
- **Planned demonstration:** Circuit/packet comparison and a labeled message journey.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Research Shannon, Baran, Davies, ARPANET contributors, Cerf and Kahn; Where Wizards Stay Up Late as secondary narrative beside original records.
- **Boundary:** No lone-inventor history or claim that packet switching alone guarantees delivery.

#### 19. From networks to a page

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** Entering the shop address starts several distinct processes.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Distinguish Internet/Web, DNS/URL, HTTP/secure transport, document/presentation/behavior and client/server.
- **Vocabulary:** DNS, URL, HTTP, HTTPS, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, browser, server.
- **Planned demonstration:** A request-to-render diagram.
- **Evidence and existing material:** CERN documents, Berners-Lee’s work, official standards and browser documentation; verify dates and attribution.
- **Boundary:** No implied equivalence between encryption and trustworthy business behavior.

#### 20. Build the whole shop

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A convincing product page ends at a nonfunctional purchase button.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Implement a linked store journey with semantic HTML, CSS and explicit simulated state; select a modest toolset.
- **Vocabulary:** static prototype, state, form, component, hosting, version control.
- **Planned demonstration:** Complete reference store and annotated code excerpts.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Existing Chapter 7 and mockups; #66; official HTML/CSS references; stack choice and production-boundary guide.
- **Boundary:** No real transactions, card collection, backend, or mandatory framework.

#### 21. Try it, break it, improve it

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A screenshot looks correct but a shopper cannot complete the journey.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Combine human observation, keyboard/accessibility checks, functional checks and end-to-end regression; explain when unit/integration tests apply.
- **Vocabulary:** test case, assertion, regression, end-to-end, unit, integration.
- **Planned demonstration:** Failure, fix and verification record.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Official testing-tool documentation and accessibility guidance; test the actual prototype.
- **Boundary:** No claim that automation proves usability or catches every accessibility issue.

### Part 6: Help the shop find its public

#### 22. How people find things

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A customer discovers the shirt without visiting the homepage first.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Explain selected eras of electronic commerce and search, platforms and AI-mediated discovery as changes in visibility and control.
- **Vocabulary:** directory, crawler, index, ranking, marketplace, discovery.
- **Planned demonstration:** Sourced timeline linked to interface artifacts.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Research pre-Web commerce, early shops, search, mobile, social and current AI; film anecdotes are leads, not historical evidence.
- **Boundary:** Avoid unsupported first-sale claims and treating Middle Men as documentary proof.

#### 23. Buying attention and earning trust

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** An advertisement promises an experience the landing page fails to deliver.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Connect PPC, social advertising and creator partnerships to audience, message, destination and measurement.
- **Vocabulary:** impression, reach, click, CTR, CPC, sponsorship, landing page.
- **Planned demonstration:** Fictional campaign-to-storefront comparison.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Current official platform definitions and relevant disclosure guidance; version-date changing details.
- **Boundary:** No invented endorsements, guaranteed ROI or real advertising spend.

#### 24. What happened—and was it worth it?

- **Opening scene (invented unless later sourced):** A shop celebrates revenue while acquisition and fulfillment consume its margin.
- **Reader capability and shirt application:** Interpret basic analytics, denominators and attribution limits; connect cost, returns and retention to decisions.
- **Vocabulary:** conversion, conversion rate, CPA, AOV, attribution, margin, fulfillment.
- **Planned demonstration:** Worked fictional economics and measurement plan.
- **Evidence and existing material:** Transparent invented inputs; current official analytics definitions; privacy-aware implementation notes.
- **Boundary:** Do not represent demonstration events as customer data or revenue as profit.

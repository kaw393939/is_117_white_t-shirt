# Layered editorial production

Parent tracker: [#73](https://github.com/kaw393939/is_117_white_t-shirt/issues/73). This plan governs an evolving manuscript, not a classroom administration system.

## What first layer means

The first layer is a complete structural draft: the promise, audience, chapter briefs, continuity rules, evidence needs, figure briefs, reference architecture and deliverable ownership. It is useful enough to review as a whole, but it does not pretend that research, illustrations, or full chapters have been completed. See [the prospectus](prospectus.md), [supporting materials](supporting-materials.md), and [issue map](issue-map.md).

## Passes and gates

| Pass | Work | Gate before proceeding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Structure | Prospectus, 24 briefs, appendix plan, asset plan, source/story register shapes, scope and dependencies | Every chapter has a purpose, demonstration, boundary and owner issue; old content has a destination. |
| 2 — Research | Claim audit, source dossiers, brand and expert cases, author anecdotes, object records, image rights | Sources support the specific claims proposed; gaps and permission restrictions are explicit. |
| 3 — Developmental draft | One chapter at a time, story-led explanation, comparisons, first-use vocabulary and provisional cross-links | Chapter delivers its reader capability; authorial argument and evidence are distinguishable; developmental review has resolved major omissions and repetition. |
| 4 — Demonstrations and technical production | Diagrams, labeled generated illustrations, reference shop, reusable examples, test scripts | Demonstrations match prose and shared fictional facts; the shop’s happy path and recovery paths work within declared simulation limits. |
| 5 — Specialist and editorial review | Historical/factual checks, technical checks, usability and accessibility review, rights check, continuity, copyedit | No unresolved evidence markers; exact claims and credits checked; reader problems corrected; tests pass. |
| 6 — Proof and release | Rendered proof, navigation/index check, mobile/print checks, production build and publication verification | Review approved; published artifact matches reviewed material; release notes and known limits recorded. |
| 7 — Maintenance | Corrections, dated platform guidance, link/media checks, reader feedback and revised editions | Updates are attributable and reviewed; significant changes get a fresh relevant review. |

Passes can overlap across chapters. A chapter may reach specialist review while another remains in research. Do not mark an entire pass complete merely because one sample passed. Produce one finished sample sequence early to calibrate depth, voice, figures and effort.

## Research and source conventions

Use primary research, practitioner work, original documents, official technical documentation and actual collection records where relevant. Wikipedia is an orientation link, not a substitute for supporting a consequential claim. Museum essays are secondary interpretation. Company statements establish what a company says; independent support may be needed for its performance claims. Films are edited arguments; attribute an interviewee’s statement rather than treating every statement as a settled fact.

Keep claims, quotations, dates, book editions/page references, artifact IDs, URLs and rights status together in a source register. A research candidate is not a verified citation. Mark unverified leads openly. Never invent an author recollection, customer interview, testimonial, result or expert endorsement. In imagined examples, do not use realistic statistics without identifying them as fictional inputs.

## Reviewable unit of work

An issue has one principal deliverable, named files or a bounded directory, dependencies, exclusions and acceptance checks. Research dossiers and assets are separate from chapter prose. Chapter issues integrate approved material. A parent tracker coordinates work and is not an instruction to implement everything in one PR. Branch, commit, PR, review, merge and publication verification follow PROJECT.md and CONTRIBUTING.md.

## Definition of completed work

A first-layer brief is complete when it states what will be taught and how it will be demonstrated, including unresolved research. A finished chapter is complete only after its evidence, demonstrations, accessibility, language, links and rendered appearance have been reviewed. A release is complete only after the reviewed PR is merged, checks pass and the live site is verified. Issue closure must reflect the deliverable, not merely the creation of another issue.

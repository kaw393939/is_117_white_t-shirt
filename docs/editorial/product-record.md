# Shared fictional product record

Status: first-layer continuity record for the worked examples. This is an invented teaching object, not a real product or customer offer.

## Control record

| Field | Fixed value for controlled comparisons |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Plain white T-shirt |
| Fabric | 100% cotton *(invented)* |
| Fabric weight | 180 gsm *(invented)* |
| Fit | Straight cut, crew neck, pre-shrunk *(invented)* |
| Care | Machine wash cold; line dry or tumble low *(invented)* |
| Origin | Cut and sewn in Portugal *(invented)* |
| Price | $28 *(invented)* |
| Base garment color | White |

These facts are a control group when the book compares brand story, copy, imagery, layout or storefront behavior. They do not establish that any real garment has these properties. Keep the invented label close to the facts wherever they appear outside a clearly marked teaching-example note.

## Permitted design variation

The shirt remains white, but the garment may carry an identifier when the exercise is about product design. Permitted locations include front, back, sleeve, hem, collar or another stated placement. Permitted variables include wordmark or symbol, typography, scale, color, image, repetition, crop, print, embroidery and blank space. Each variation needs a stated intention and should be judged by its relationship to the brand, wearer, context and production constraints.

The book must distinguish two experiments:

1. **Presentation control:** the garment and control facts stay fixed while copy, photography, framing, layout or interaction changes.
2. **Product variation:** the garment’s identifier, marking, construction or other property changes and the change is named.

## Supplemental narrative claims

Copy may introduce invented story claims such as “sink-tested,” “dries overnight,” “free repairs,” or “same shirt next year.” These are not part of the control record. They must be labeled as fictional brand claims and may not be used as evidence, testimonials or established product performance. When a comparison requires identical product facts, keep supplemental claims out of the facts block or label the changed claim explicitly.

## Continuity rules

- Keep the control record identical in the three current dossiers and comparison wherever the text says “same shirt” or “identical product.”
- A brand’s logo or identifier may change the product; record that change rather than treating it as mere presentation.
- The color white is the shared material constraint, not a ban on marks.
- Personas describe hypothesized customers; archetypes describe brand story patterns. Do not merge those terms.
- All brands, people, prices, policies, factories, measurements and product claims in these examples are invented unless a page explicitly cites outside evidence.
- If a chapter needs a different product fact for a technology or business exercise, give it a new fictional record ID instead of modifying this control record.

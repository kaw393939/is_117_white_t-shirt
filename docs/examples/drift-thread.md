# Dossier: Drift & Thread

> A complete worked example. Drift & Thread is an **invented brand** created for this book. Same shirt as [Standard Issue](standard-issue.md), different story — which is the entire point.

## Archetype card

- **Primary archetype: Explorer.** The shirt as the thing you pack without thinking. Freedom through lightness; identity through where you've been, not what you own.
- **Accent: Sage.** The Explorer who also reads the label — fabric weight matters because sink-washing matters. Knowledge serves the road.
- **Failure mode to avoid:** borrowed ruggedness. Adventure performed for people who mostly commute becomes costume. The antidote is practicality: every claim must survive an actual trip.
- **Persona sketch (invented):** a one-bag traveler who counts grams and layovers; wants one shirt that dries by morning; distrusts anything described as "essential luxury."

## Voice sheet

1. Second person, present tense. The reader is already moving.
2. Sensory and specific: night bus, sink, line-dry, dawn. Never "wanderlust."
3. Sentences can stretch a little — the road is not a bullet point.
4. Warmth, not hype. The shirt is a companion, not a trophy.
5. Facts stay factual. A traveler checks the label before trusting the story.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Wash it in a sink. Wear it by morning." | "Unlock your spirit of adventure." |
| "One shirt for the overnight train and the meeting after it." | "For the modern nomad who demands the best." |
| "180 gsm: opaque in daylight, dries overnight." | "Premium travel-ready performance luxury." |

- **Type:** a humanist sans for text — warm, legible at small sizes — with a serif reserved for place names and headlines. Nothing geometric; this brand has no grid to worship.
- **Layout:** still orderly (a traveler needs to find things), but margins breathe asymmetrically and images may bleed to the edge. Order in service of the journey, not order for its own sake.
- **Imagery:** warm light, creased fabric, motion blur allowed. The shirt photographed *in use* — over a hostel chair, on a train window hook. Props earn their place: a map, a ticket, one bag.

## Product copy

**Hero line:** The shirt that's already packed.

**Description (60 words):**

> Some shirts live in drawers. This one lives in your bag. The midweight cotton handles a night bus and a morning meeting, washes clean in a sink, and dries by the time the coffee's ready. One shirt for the whole itinerary — the map is optional, the laundry isn't. White goes with everything you didn't pack.

**Facts (identical product, invented for the example):** 100% cotton, 180 gsm · straight cut, crew neck, pre-shrunk · machine wash cold · cut and sewn in Portugal · $28.

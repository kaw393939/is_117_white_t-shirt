# One brief, three answers

> All three brands below are **invented for this book**. The product facts are identical and fictional. What diverges is everything the facts don't cover.

The brief never changed: sell a plain white T-shirt — 100% cotton, 180 gsm, straight cut, made in Portugal, $28. Three teams answered it. Watch the same object become three different products without a single product fact moving.

## What the comparison teaches

**Choices compose.** The archetype drives the voice; the voice drives the copy; the archetype plus a design tradition drive the page. Change the top of the stack and everything below it moves — which is why Chapter 3 insisted on choosing the story first.

**The facts are the control group.** Every facts section describes the same invented shirt in the same terms. That constancy is the experiment's design: whatever differs between the three pages is presentation, and only presentation. Chapter 1's discipline — change the story freely, never falsify the shirt — is what makes the comparison honest.

**No answer is the answer.** Standard Issue would bore the Blank Canvas customer; Blank Canvas would exhaust the Standard Issue customer; Drift & Thread speaks to someone planning a trip. The brief is the same. The audience decides which answer is correct — which is why a brand starts with who it's for.

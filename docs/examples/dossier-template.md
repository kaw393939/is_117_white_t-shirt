# The blank dossier

> Copy this page. Fill every bracket with a decision you can defend. Three filled-in examples — [Standard Issue](standard-issue.md), [Drift & Thread](drift-thread.md), [Blank Canvas Co.](blank-canvas.md) — show what "done" looks like. Their comparison is [here](comparison.md).

## The rules of the exercise

1. **Choose your own archetype.** Explorer, Jester, and Everyperson are taken by the examples. Sage, Caregiver, Magician, Rebel, Creator, a blend of two — anything you can argue for.
2. **Keep the product facts fixed.** Same invented shirt: 100% cotton, 180 gsm, straight cut, crew neck, pre-shrunk, made in Portugal, $28. The facts are the control group; your story is the variable.
3. **Everything you invent, label invented.**
4. **The sequence is the skill.** Archetype first, voice second, visuals third, copy fourth, page last. Each layer must be defensible in terms of the one above it.

The [shared product record](../editorial/product-record.md) gives the complete fictional facts and care instructions. A white shirt may carry an identifier on its front, back, sleeve or hem. If you change its markings, call the exercise a product variation and explain the placement, scale and technique. For a presentation-only comparison, keep the garment itself fixed.

## Storefront

Build the page. Hero, navigation, one call to action, facts, reassurance, FAQ, footer — semantic HTML, the brand tokens as CSS values, no fake urgency. The [three example storefronts](comparison.md) are the bar.

## The final check

Hand your dossier to a partner who hasn't heard your pitch. Ask them what the brand is, who it's for, and whether they'd trust it. If their answers match your intent, the system works. If not — that's the revision, and the whole point of writing it down.
